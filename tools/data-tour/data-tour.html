Part 1 · Literally

Four spreadsheets

Everything we store boils down to four tables. If you opened them in Excel, these are the columns you'd see. The rows below are real.

1. Raw listings, straight from Blizzard

Every hour Blizzard hands us one long list per auction house: about 100,000 rows for a busy one. One row is one listing someone posted. We keep these for 7 days. Gear (left) and stackable goods (right) come from two separate lists.

id
The listing's own number. If it vanishes before its time runs out, it probably sold.
item_id
Which item. Just a number; names come from table 3.
bonus
Which version of the item ("of the Eagle", etc.). Blank for most items. Names come from table 4.
buyout / unit_price
Price in copper. Gear lists a total buyout; stackable goods list a price per unit.
time_left
Only a rough bucket: SHORT, MEDIUM, LONG or VERY_LONG. No exact times, and no seller names.
What it looks like before it's a table (JSON)

    


    

      

        
2. The hourly summary, which is what analysis reads

        
We boil each hour's ~1 million listings down to one row per house × item × version: how many are listed and what they cost. About 107,000 rows an hour, kept for 90 days.

      

      

      

        
listings / quantity
How many separate listings, and how many units in total (a stack of 20 cloth = 1 listing, 20 units).

        
min_price
The cheapest unit on sale right now.

        
p10 / p25 / median
Walk up the price ladder from the cheapest unit: p10 is the price once you've passed 10% of all units, median at 50%. Robust against a few silly listings.

        
max_price
The most expensive listing. Often a joke or a mistake (see the fish below).

Part 2 · Figuratively

      
Ways to slice it

      
The same tables, viewed as a market. Each chart below is one possible view for the app, using the 03:17 snapshot unless it says otherwise. Hover or tap a mark for exact numbers.

    


    

      

        
The whole market, by category

        
"Market size" here = units listed × each item's typical price, across all 15 houses. It's the closest thing to a market cap we have, since we can't see actual sales.

      

      

        
Gold listed

        
Number of listings

      

      
Gear is many small sales; materials are a busy market. Armor and Trade Goods are similar in gold, but Trade Goods has only 398 different items to Armor's 3,527, so each material trades in volume. Recipes are few but valuable.

    


    

      

        
Raw materials vs. gear

        
The API itself splits the market in two: stackable goods (herbs, ore, cloth, potions: one shared price per unit) and individual items (gear, recipes: each listing priced on its own).

      

      
    


    

      

        
Houses and factions

        
Each faction has two big houses, two small ones and one empty one. Blackwater (neutral) is a fraction of either.

Same item, different house, different price

        
Typical price in each house. Houses within a faction behave like separate markets, so gaps between them are trading opportunities.

The price ladder (market depth)

          
Black Lotus, Horde house 113: how many you could buy up to each price.

        

        
        
A "wall" of 30-odd lotus at 15 g, then it thins out fast. Shows how much you can buy before the price climbs, which is the key question before cornering a market.

      

    


    

      

        
One item, many versions

        
Archer's Cloak in the two big Horde houses: typical price of each "of the …" version (bonus ID shown; the app would show the suffix name).

      

      
      
From 0.24 g to 2.93 g for the same cloak, a 12× spread by suffix alone. Pricing versions separately was worth it.

Part 3 · For the app

      
Views worth building

      
Ideas for the dashboard (#11), and whether today's data can already support them.

    

    

      
Data ready
Deals board
Listings priced well below the item's typical price in that house, with supply alongside so thin markets don't fool you.

      
Data ready
Cross-house gaps
Items cheaper in one house than another of the same faction. Today: Black Lotus, Arcanite Bar, Runecloth.

      
Data ready
Market depth
The price ladder for any item: how much is cheap, where the walls are, what it would cost to buy out.

      
Data ready
Version premiums
Which "of the …" suffixes sell high for each piece of gear, so you know which drops to list and which to vendor.

      
Needs days
Movers
Biggest price and supply changes over 24 hours and 7 days: floods to buy into, shortages to sell into.

      
Needs a week+
Time-of-day patterns
A heatmap of price by hour and weekday for each material, to time buying and selling. This is the main use for the hourly data (#33).

      
Needs recipes
Crafting margins
Material cost vs sale price for what you can craft. Needs recipe data and your professions.

      
Needs a method
Sale rate
How often an item actually sells, inferred from listings disappearing between hours (#10). Separates good flips from traps.
